# Coverage audit: countries, reports and editions

*3 and 4 October 2026. GOV.UK was read on 3 October (20:03 to 20:17 UTC) and checked again on 4 October (07:15 UTC). The Internet Archive catalogue was read on 4 October (07:11 UTC), when it covered 37 of the 47 countries.*

The question was: have we scraped every relevant document, including archived versions, and are we missing any countries or reports?

## The short answer

**Are we missing countries?** Not from today's collection. GOV.UK's collection lists 47 country pages and all 47 are held. But twelve more countries were in the collection in the past, and nothing of them is held. Seven of those pages are still on GOV.UK, marked as withdrawn, and list 17 PDFs between them (Angola, Cameroon, Liberia, Malawi, Mali, North Korea, Rwanda). Five have been taken down (Botswana, Mauritius, Moldova, Morocco, South Africa). The scraper never sees any of the twelve, because it starts from the collection's live list.

**Are we missing reports?** No, not of what GOV.UK publishes today. The 47 pages list 164 web versions and 175 PDFs. All 339 are held. Eleven editions are published as a PDF only, and the text of all eleven has been taken from the PDF. No page lists any other kind of file. All 164 web texts were byte for byte the same as GOV.UK's on 3 October, and no page had changed by the morning of 4 October.

**Are we missing editions (the history)?** Yes. This is the real gap. The site holds 306 editions of 197 reports: the 175 current ones and 131 older ones from the Internet Archive. For the 37 countries catalogued so far, archived copies of the country pages show 388 more editions that are not held. 286 of them can be recovered from the Internet Archive as PDFs. 102 are not in the Internet Archive. Almost all of the 388 (382) were only ever published as PDFs, which is why the earlier hunt for archived web pages did not find them. 52 of them were still on GOV.UK in 2024 or later. The other ten countries have not been catalogued, so their number is unknown.

**Anything else?** One document linked from the collection page is not held: "About country policy and information notes", the Home Office's own account of the notes' purpose, methodology and use (published 3 June 2026).

**Are the archived reports in The National Archives?** Very likely some are, but this audit could not confirm it. The National Archives does archive GOV.UK: its own list of archived websites says so. But its web archive tells automated tools to stay out (its robots.txt disallows everything). The project's rule is to respect that, so nothing was asked of it. A person can look by hand in a browser, and a list of addresses to try is in section E2.

## E2. The National Archives' web archive

The owner asked whether the archived reports are in The National Archives. So far the project has used only the Internet Archive.

**Plain answer:** probably yes, at least in part. This audit could not confirm it, and could not measure it. The archive refuses automated requests, and the project's rule is to respect that.

### What was done

1. **Its robots.txt was read** (the file a website uses to tell automated tools what they may fetch). At `webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk` it says that every automated tool is disallowed from every address, and that one named commercial crawler is allowed. The project's client reads this file before any request and obeys it. On 3 October it refused to send the one question I tried. Nothing but the robots.txt file has been requested from the archive: no page, no list of captures, no file.
2. **The National Archives' own pages about the archive were read**: eleven pages on its main website, where its robots.txt allows this. They say four things that matter here.
   - Its A to Z list of archived websites has 9,372 entries. GOV.UK itself (`https://www.gov.uk/`) is one. So are the Home Office's pages on GOV.UK and the old UK Border Agency website, where the pre-2014 guidance notes and country reports were published. The file address that holds GOV.UK's PDFs (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk`) is not listed as a site of its own.
   - The "About" page says the archive is open to web search engines and follows the Memento standard (a common way of asking an archive for its captures of an address). This does not agree with the robots.txt. I followed the robots.txt.
   - The "Policy on use of website and catalogue data" says automated users must name themselves honestly, must follow the robots.txt of the site they are reading, must keep to a modest rate, and should write to the Archives before acting if the robots.txt would prevent what they need.
   - The re-use page says Crown copyright material from the archive can be re-used under the Open Government Licence, the same licence the notes already carry.
3. No way round was tried. A third-party service that asks the archive on your behalf, or a browser driven by a program, would be a way round a refusal.

### The four questions

**1. Does it hold the collection page, the country pages, the note pages and the PDFs?** Verified: it archives GOV.UK as a site, with repeated captures. Not checked: whether it has any capture of the collection page, a country page, a note or a PDF. Pages sampled: 0 of the 9 planned (the collection page and eight countries). Captures of the collection and country pages are likely, because they are ordinary GOV.UK pages. Whether it keeps the PDFs is not known: they sit on a separate file address, and that address is not on its list.

**2. How does it list captures?** Three documented ways, none usable by an automated tool while the robots.txt stands.
   - A **timeline page** for any address: a calendar of every capture. Its address is `https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukgwa/timeline/` followed by the live address. This comes from the Archives' own "Check the web archive" bookmark. (Its A to Z list uses `timeline1/` in the same place.)
   - A **search** at `https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/search/`, which can be narrowed to one website and one year, and whose results (the first 10,000) can be exported as a spreadsheet or data file. The export has the capture time, the address, the file type, a fingerprint and the size. That is the same kind of list the Internet Archive gives.
   - **Memento**, as the "About" page says. The pages I read give no address for it.

**3. Does it have the editions the Internet Archive lacks?** Not checked. Editions sampled: 0 of the 24 prepared. The list is below so that a person can check it by hand.

**4. What would it take to add it as a second source?**
   - **Permission comes first.** Write to the Web Archiving Team (`webarchive@nationalarchives.gov.uk`), as their policy asks, saying what the tool is, how little it would ask (lists of captures for about 60 GOV.UK addresses and a few hundred files, one request every second or two), and asking either to be allowed in the robots.txt or to be given the lists another way. Until they agree, the project's rules do not allow the tool to use it.
   - **A route that needs no permission:** a person can run the archive's search in a browser and export the results. The project could read that exported file offline and say exactly which missing editions the archive holds. This is hand work by the owner, not scraping.
   - **Then the code.** The present code (`src/cpin/wayback.py` and `src/cpin/recover.py`, read, not changed) is written around the Internet Archive in five places: the address of its index and the way questions are put to it; the address form of a capture "as recorded", which is what lets a note's text be stored word for word; its fingerprint for each capture, used to spot the same file under two addresses and to check a downloaded PDF; the label `source: wayback` on every recovered version, which the site shows as "archived copy" and uses in citations; and the catalogue's layout. A second archive needs its own small module for the first three, its own label (the project's rule is that every copy says where it came from), a column in the catalogue saying which archive holds what, the site's wording and citations, the host's delay in the settings, and tests. One thing must be checked before any of it: whether the archive can return a page exactly as captured. If it only returns pages with its own links written in, a note's links could not be kept as published.

No PDF and no archive capture was downloaded. Nothing under `data/`, `src/`, `prototypes/`, `tests/` or `web/` was changed by this audit, apart from `data/verify-report.json`, which `verify` writes. Working files were kept outside the repository.
- **Requests made:** GOV.UK content interface, 75 (the collection twice, the 47 country pages, the "About" page, the 12 former country pages, 11 other pages, 2 notes). GOV.UK search, 69. `verify --live`, 164 notes. Internet Archive index, 5 questions. The National Archives' main website, 11 pages. The National Archives' web archive, its robots.txt only.

- **B. Reports:** each page's live list of attachments compared with the stored note (text on disk, fingerprint recomputed), the PDF list (file on disk, size against GOV.UK's figure) and the PDF text store.
- **C. Currency:** `./cpin verify --live`, the run log `data/runs.jsonl`, and a fresh reading of the collection on 4 October.
- **D. Editions:** the site's own data (`prototypes/data/series/`, exported 4 October at 07:12 UTC) run through the site's own timeline code, so that "update with no edition held" means here what it means on the site; version numbers from the stored notes; the catalogue as it stood, copied before reading. Listings were grouped into distinct editions by report and month.
- **Hand checks of the most surprising findings:**
  1. *An edition marked as recoverable.* Afghanistan, Hazaras, August 2018: the Internet Archive's index, asked directly, shows one good capture (24 July 2019) with the fingerprint the catalogue records. The store holds no copy. Confirmed.
  2. *An edition marked as not archived.* Afghanistan, Hindus and Sikhs, January 2016: the index, asked directly under both of GOV.UK's addresses for the file, returns nothing. Confirmed.
  3. *Recent editions marked as not held and not archived.* Three were looked at closely. Brazil, internal relocation, August 2025, is a real gap.   4. *The withdrawn pages.* Each of the seven was read on GOV.UK: the withdrawal notice, date and attachments are as listed in Gap 1. The store has no folder for any of them.
- **Counting choices:** "held" for a web version means the stored text is on disk and matches its fingerprint. "Edition" means one version number in one month. "Listing" means one document as an archived country page showed it. Dates of archive listings are the dates of the archive's visits, not of publication.
