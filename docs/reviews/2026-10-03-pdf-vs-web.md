# Web version against PDF: what differs between the two (3 October 2026)

The Home Office publishes each edition of a note twice on GOV.UK: as a web page and as a PDF. The owner asked
what a reader of one would miss, or be misled about, compared with the other. This compares all 164 current
notes that have both, and keeps two things apart throughout: **real differences between the two publications**,
and **artefacts of our own reading of the PDF** (`src/cpin/pdftext.py`), which are not differences at all.

## The short answer

**"10% is quite a lot different." It is not 10%, or anything like it.** The 90.6% figure was our PDF
extraction dropping an unbookmarked Preface (since fixed), not a difference between the publications. For that
note (Ghana, medical treatment and healthcare) the web version and the PDF differ by **0 of 15,854 words**.

Measured word by word over all text (paragraphs, headings, lists, table cells, footnotes; 3.86 million words on
each side), with every difference checked against the PDF's own raw text:

| How much of a note's wording really differs | best | middle note | worst tenth | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine wording (words in one and not the other, or changed) | 0 (49 notes) | 0.011% (5 words) | 0.18% (94 words) | **4.4%** |
| The same, plus redaction notices and addresses shown as text | 0 (27 notes) | 0.031% | 0.28% | 4.4% |
| Everything real, including paragraph numbers, list numbers and the words the web adds for pictures | 0 (13 notes) | 0.18% (72 words) | 0.81% (332 words) | 4.4% |

Only 3 notes differ in wording by 0.5% or more, 2 by 1% or more, and none by 5%. The worst cases, with real word
counts:

- **Vietnam, unaccompanied children (v3.0, February 2025): 4.4%.** 654 words only on the web, 1,405 only in the
  PDF. The web page is labelled version 3.0 but still carries version 2.0's text in places: every one of the
  web-only passages of five words or more (462 words) is in the archived copy of the web's own version 2.0, and
  the PDF's new paragraphs (in 11.1 and 13.1, and 14.3.3 and 15.1.3) and 13 extra footnotes are not on the web.
- **Iraq, religious minorities (v4.0, September 2024): 1.1%.** The web version prints paragraphs 3.1.9 to 3.1.14
  of the Assessment twice (811 words); the PDF has them once.
- **Iraq, blood feuds, honour crimes and tribal violence (v1.0, July 2024): 0.5%.** The PDF's Assessment has
  paragraphs 4.3.4 to 4.3.8 (about 230 words); the web version goes from 4.3.3 straight to 4.4.
- **China, opposition to the state (v5.0, January 2026): 0.5%.** The PDF's Assessment has a section 3.5 "Family
  members of perceived opponents of the state" (three paragraphs, 209 words); the web version has no 3.5.
- The notes that looked worst before pictures were set aside are not wording differences: China, family planning
  (1,894 web words: two emails and a table that the PDF shows as pictures and the web types out); Nigeria,
  internal relocation (2,311) and Kenya, actors of protection (816): words the web adds for charts, mostly
  tables of their numbers; Vietnam, Hoa Hao (578) and Bangladesh, documentation (425): the PDF repeats the
  "information removed" notice for each withheld paragraph and the web prints it once.

**What does differ, in the order it matters to someone relying on one version:**

1. **The two really say different things somewhere in 115 notes**; in 75 of them by a passage of four words or
   more, usually a sentence or less. The table below lists the 29 that matter most. The pattern is nearly always
   the same: the web version has lost the opening or closing words of a paragraph, a bullet point, or a few
   paragraphs, that the PDF has. 35 notes have at least one such difference inside the Assessment or Executive
   summary (60 places; 28 of them passages, in 20 notes). Six notes carry signs that the web page and the PDF are
   different drafts under the same version number (Vietnam unaccompanied children; Georgia SOGIE; Turkey PKK, Kurds
   and military service; Syria criticism of the government).
2. **Paragraph numbers differ in 74 notes.** 494 paragraphs in 61 notes have a different number on the web from
   the PDF; three notes have no paragraph numbers on the web at all (540 paragraphs in 33 notes are numbered in the
   PDF only). A citation "paragraph 18.4.1" can point at different text in the two versions.
3. **Pictures.** The PDFs hold at least 220 more pictures than the web versions (638 against 418, in 57 notes);
   22 notes have none on the web. No note has more on the web. In place of pictures the web sometimes adds a table
   of a chart's numbers (97 tables in 37 notes) or a sentence describing a map (102 passages in 42 notes): words
   the PDF does not have. In 36 places (17 notes) the web text says "the map below" or similar and shows nothing.
4. **Dates of currency.** The "Section updated: <date>" lines are the same in 151 notes. In 10 the web version has
   none of the PDF's; in two the date differs (Georgia SOGIE: 2023 on the web, 2024 in the PDF; Iraq actors of
   protection: 25 July on the web, 25 June in the PDF). The version number never differs (140 of 140 where both
   show one); 11 web versions show no version number at the top and 10 more show it without the month.
5. **Footnotes.** The count is the same in 157 notes. In 7 the PDF has more (22 footnotes in all), so from the
   missing one onwards the web's footnote numbers run behind the PDF's.
6. **Withheld ("Official – sensitive") sections.** 479 boxes in the PDFs, 472 on the web: 6 notes have fewer
   on the web. In 31 notes the PDF shows how much was withheld (one notice per paragraph, often with its number)
   where the web shows a single notice.
7. **Headings.** 142 headings in 53 notes are worded or numbered differently; 32 of them are cut short on the web.
8. **Links.** 108 notes link to exactly the same places. In the rest, 127 link targets are on the web only and 124
   in the PDF only; 31 of the PDF-only targets and 6 of the web-only ones are Home Office internal addresses
   (SharePoint, Outlook "safelinks") where the other version usually gives the public address.

**What turned out to be nothing:** the two are always the same edition by version number and "valid from" date;
bullets, list nesting and quotation marks differ only in form; the PDF's cover, contents list, page numbers and
7,032 "Back to Contents" lines have no counterpart on the web and no content.

**Practical upshot.** Neither version is reliably the fuller one, but where they differ the PDF is usually the
complete text and the web version the damaged copy (cut sentences, dropped paragraphs, missing pictures, missing
numbers). The web is fuller only where it repeats itself or spells out a picture. For a quotation or a paragraph
reference that matters, check the PDF. This review does not establish which version the Home Office treats as
authoritative.

## How it was measured

- **The pairs.** The 164 current notes with both a web version and a mirrored PDF (`pair_pdfs`, as `./cpin pdftext
  --check` enumerates them). Web side: the stored body exactly as GOV.UK returned it. PDF side: two independent
  readings, (a) the extraction (`pdftext.pdf_to_html`, snapshot of "pdftext-2" taken at 18:38 on 3 October, sha256
  `cdf5edc2…`) and (b) the PDF's own raw data read directly with PyMuPDF: `page.get_text()`, bookmarks, link
  annotations, embedded images, and each line's typeface and size.
- **Words.** Every block of text on both sides (paragraphs, list items, headings, table cells, captions,
  footnotes) was reduced to its words and numbers; footnote marks and "↩" links were left out; `<br>` counts as a
  space. Total: 3,858,279 web words, 3,847,731 PDF words (the PDF count leaves out the cover, contents, page numbers
  and "Back to Contents").
- **Step 1, diff.** A word-level diff of web against extraction gave 3,785 places where they differ.
- **Step 2, confirm against the raw PDF.** For each place, the web's wording with its neighbouring words was
  searched for in the PDF's raw text (three readings: as it comes; with raised footnote numbers taken out; with
  body text run on across page ends). If the PDF's raw text has the web's wording there, the difference is ours,
  not theirs. If instead the raw text has the extraction's wording, it is a real difference. Repeated sentences are
  handled by counting (the PDF must have a passage at least as often as the web does). Beside tables the test is
  looser: the words of each web row or cell found together on one PDF page, in any order.
- **Result.** 2,671 real differences, 1,058 artefacts of the extraction, 31 the same words in a different place,
  25 unresolved (114 words in all). 5 notes have no difference of any kind; 13 have no real one.
- **Step 3, the other direction.** Every line of every PDF's raw text was checked for presence in the extraction
  and in the web version, to find PDF text the extractor drops. What is in neither: page numbers, "Back to
  Contents", the contents pages, the covers, and chart lettering (285 lines in 21 notes). The remaining 76 lines
  were all explained (superscript "m²", footnote numbers). No body text of a PDF is missing from both.
- **Structure** (headings, numbering, footnotes, links, boxes, pictures, tables, lists) was compared from the web
  body against the PDF's raw data where the raw data can say (bookmarks, link annotations, images, lines), and
  against the extraction otherwise; each section below says which.
- Scripts, caches and per-pair output are in the session's scratch folder (`pdfweb/`), not in the repo;
  `perpair.csv` there has the word counts for each of the 164 pairs.

## The numbers, by kind of difference

### 1. Wording

Words in real differences, all 164 pairs:

| Kind | Only on the web | Only in the PDF | Notes affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine wording: running text, headings, footnotes, bibliography, table cells, "Section updated" lines | 2,705 | 4,065 | 115 |
| Redaction notices | 35 | 2,668 | 34 |
| Addresses shown as text (an email or URL in place of its link words) | 337 | 524 | 60 |
| Words standing in for a picture: tables of a chart's numbers (5,420), descriptions and lettering (6,882), captions (427) | 12,729 | 17 | 65 |
| Paragraph and list numbers, spacing, repeated labels, stray characters, version line | 839 | 2,685 | 110 |
| *Artefacts of the extraction (not differences)* | *4,583* | *2,302* | |

The 779 genuine wording differences by size: 312 passages of four words or more (75 notes), 293 of one to three
words (71 notes), 112 spellings (46 notes; e.g. the web's "marginalisatoin", "Tbisili", "Material fats"; the PDF's
"Aprl", "facilties"), 62 numbers or dates (25 notes). By place: country information 357 (94 notes), bibliography
144, Assessment and Executive summary 60 (35 notes; 1,675 words, of which 811 are the Iraq repeat), footnotes 53,
back matter 23, annexes and other 142.

Without Vietnam unaccompanied children: 2,051 web-only and 2,660 PDF-only words in 163 notes.

The main session's own count, which treats words the web types out for a picture as web text and ignores order,
gave "web text the PDF lacks: median 0.04%, worst 7.2%; PDF words the web lacks: median 0.06%, worst 4.7%". That
agrees with this audit: 7.2% is China family planning (1,691 of 23,471 words: the two emails), which is classed
here as picture-related. On this audit's count the PDF-only worst is 5.9% (Vietnam unaccompanied children, 1,405
of 23,650 words, bibliography and footnotes included).

**Typographic only (not counted above).** Among 3,834,558 matching words: 6,272 places differ only in the style
of quote or dash (GOV.UK turns straight quotes into curly ones: the PDFs have 4,384 straight single and 1,970
straight double quotes, the web 2 and 0; and "..." into "…"). The web has 37,218 non-breaking spaces, the PDF text
none. 595 words differ only in capitals (mostly "Accessed"/"accessed" in bibliographies and "GOV.UK"/"gov.uk").
Each side has six ligature characters ("ﬁ"). An exact phrase copied from one version may therefore not be
found by search in the other where it contains an apostrophe or quotation mark.

**Structural (same words, different breaks).** About 2,900 places have a paragraph or cell break on one side
only, and about 6,100 differ in punctuation; both are dominated by the bibliography (the web makes each source name
a heading and drops it from the entries) and by tables. These were counted, not confirmed one by one against the
raw PDF, and some are certainly the extraction's joins at page ends.

### 2. Paragraph and section numbers

Confirmed against the raw PDF (number and the words after it found together):

- 74 notes have at least one numbering difference; 77 have exactly the same set of paragraph numbers.
- **Different number**: 494 paragraphs in 61 notes, and 38 headings. Largest: Iraq blood feuds (150 paragraphs, 25
  headings: the web leaves "Legal context" unnumbered, so the web's sections 8 to 11 are the PDF's 9 to 12);
  Eritrea national service (33: the PDF numbers two sections 12.1 and two 12.2, and 18.1 twice; the web runs on
  12.5, 12.6, 18.2 to 18.6); Iraq religious minorities (22); India religious minorities (18); Colombia armed groups
  (16); Iran Kurds (16).
- **Numbered in the PDF, not on the web**: 540 paragraphs in 33 notes. Three notes have no paragraph numbers on
  the web at all: Iran, early and forced marriage (218); Pakistan, healthcare (138); Iran, honour-based violence
  (129).
- **Numbered on the web, not in the PDF**: 12.
- Duplicate paragraph numbers within one version: 61 web versions, 38 PDFs.
- 209 list items carry a typed "1.", "a)" or "ii)" in the PDF where the web has none, and 11 the reverse (39
  notes); 258 web list items are numbered automatically, so a PDF's "A., B." can appear as other numbers on the
  web (Syria Alawites 13.2.18).

### 3. Headings

Web headings against the PDF's bookmarks, then checked against the words on the PDF's pages:

- 12,843 web headings, 8,420 PDF bookmarks (5 PDFs have none); 8,271 the same, in order.
- **Only on the web: 4,429.** 2,951 are bibliography source names (known). Of the other 1,478 (160 notes), 1,426
  are in the PDF as an ordinary or bold line, not a bookmark: "Clearance" and "Changes from last version of this
  note" in nearly every note, and question headings in fact-finding reports. Vietnam opposition to the state turns
  a PDF table (Annex A) into 160 headings ("Occupation", "Date arrested" repeated for each person).
- **Only in the PDF: 186 bookmarks in 81 notes**; 138 are on the web as plain text, the rest are bookmark debris
  ("Untitled").
- **Worded or numbered differently: 142 in 53 notes**, all confirmed on the PDF page: 58 differ in the number only,
  32 are cut short on the web ("12.6 Forced marriage" for "12.6 Forced marriage and child marriage"), 52 differ in
  words ("16.4 Discrimination and legal rights" for "Documentation and legal rights", Burma; "3.4 Treatment of
  Amhara political elites" for "political parties", Ethiopia; "Asessment", Iran).
- Level: 2,166 headings in 101 notes sit at a different depth on the web from the PDF's bookmark outline. This
  affects navigation only and was not checked against the printed size of the headings.

### 4. Footnotes

- 31,428 on the web, 31,450 in the PDFs. Same count in 157 notes. **PDF has more in 7**: Vietnam unaccompanied
  children (124 against 111), China medical (343/339), Ghana internal relocation (327/326: the PDF's footnote 316),
  Iran Kurds bulletin (64/63: footnote 20), Malaysia SOGIE (236/235: 117), Sri Lanka SOGIE (216/215: 166), Syria
  military service (65/64: 60). After the missing footnote the same number means a different source.
- Every web footnote is referenced once in the web text (31,437 marks; none unreferenced).
- Text by number: 31,192 the same; 53 real differences of wording in 26 notes (115 web words, 483 PDF words), plus
  the shifted ones above. Examples: Bangladesh religious minorities, "(page 67)" on the web, "(page 68)" in the
  PDF; Eritrea footnote 86 on the web reads "Eritrea](https://www.state.gov/…)" where the PDF has "USSD, 2025
  Trafficking in Persons Report: Eritrea".
- After footnotes 100 and up, one web version (Bangladesh medical) shows stray "(00)", "(01)" … in the text (13
  places).
- Links in footnotes: 364 footnotes in 59 notes have a different target; in 55 the PDF footnote has a link and the
  web's has none, in 32 the reverse.

### 5. Links

- Web: 58,824 links (52,580 external: 22,446 in the text, 30,134 in footnotes; 5,956 within the page; 288 email).
  PDF: 60,374 external link annotations and 48,311 internal ones (contents entries, "Back to Contents", footnote
  marks).
- Distinct external targets: 20,689 web, 20,686 PDF. **108 notes link to exactly the same set.** After ignoring
  trailing slashes and full stops: 127 targets only on the web (43 notes), 124 only in the PDF (42 notes).
- Same link words, different target: 53 links in 27 notes. 10 differ by a character or two; 27 go to another page
  of the same site; 16 to another site.
- Internal addresses published as links: in the PDFs, 11 Outlook "safelinks" wrappers (6 notes) and 20 Home Office
  SharePoint addresses (10 notes); on the web, 6 SharePoint addresses (5 notes). The other version usually has the
  public address. Example: Afghanistan fear of the Taliban, "Amnesty International" goes to amnesty.org on the web
  and to a safelinks wrapper in the PDF.
- Wrong target on the web: Nigeria internal relocation, five route links ("Kano City … to Abuja", "Ibadan … to
  Port Harcourt" and others) go to an Abuja–Lagos or Lagos–Port Harcourt map on the web; the PDF's go to the
  routes named.
- The feedback address: 48 web versions print "cipu@homeoffice.gov.uk" where the PDF prints "Country Policy and
  Information Team" over the same link.
- 12 web versions show a link's raw markup as text ("…Persons Report: Eritrea](https://…)"), and 9 links in 3
  notes carry GOV.UK's "(PDF, 173KB)" label, which the PDF does not have.

### 6. Tables

- 526 tables on the web; the extractor finds 435 in the PDFs.
- 340 web tables match a PDF table; 12 match PDF text the extractor did not see as a table.
- **97 web tables in 37 notes have no counterpart in the PDF's text** (5,420 words): they give the numbers of a
  chart the PDF shows as a picture. A further 77 web tables could not be matched in order (a PDF table the
  extraction scrambled, or a picture with lettering); these were not separated.
- **35 PDF tables in 16 notes are not tables on the web**: paragraphs, lists or headings instead (drug lists in the
  Ghana and China medical notes; Vietnam opposition Annex A).
- Cell values: 39 real differences in 11 notes, all labels or layout (the web repeats the year in each row of
  Afghanistan fear of the Taliban 13.1.3; "Number of incidents in 2022" for a PDF column headed "2022"). No changed
  figure was found in a table that both versions have as text. Two tables lack rows or are absent on the web: see
  Sudan and Ethiopia in the table of differences.
- Limit: where a PDF table could only be compared as a bag of words, a changed number could be missed.

### 7. Pictures

- Web: 418 pictures, none with alternative text, 33 with a caption. PDFs: 638 by the extractor's count (pictures
  and drawn charts), 572 embedded images by the raw count (cover excluded).
- PDF has more in 57 notes (220 more in total); the same number in 107 (53 of them have none); the web never has
  more. 22 notes have none on the web and 97 between them in the PDF. Largest gaps: Yemen humanitarian (25 against
  0), Vietnam ethnic and religious groups (22/1), Yemen security and humanitarian (16/0), Syria humanitarian
  (24/10), Nigeria internal relocation (21/11), Kenya actors of protection (13/3), China family planning (10/0).
- The web text points to a map, chart, graph, image or diagram "below" or "above" 370 times. 254 have a web picture
  beside them; 80 have a table instead; **36 in 17 notes have neither** (Yemen humanitarian 11, Yemen security 5,
  Malaysia SOGIE 3, where the web says "[this chart is available in the PDF version…]").
- Words the web adds in place of or beside a picture: 12,729 (above).

### 8. "Official – sensitive: not for disclosure" boxes

- Counted from the raw PDF text and the web body: 479 boxes in 162 PDFs, 472 in 161 web versions.
- **6 notes have fewer on the web.** The box at the end of "1.2 Exclusion" is missing in China Christians, China
  Hong Kong national security, Ethiopia Amhara and Namibia women; China medical has 2 in the PDF and none on the
  web; Namibia SOGIE lacks the one under Clearance (and the clearance details with it).
- **Notices inside the boxes: 623 in the PDFs, 483 on the web.** In 31 notes the PDF has more, because it repeats
  the notice for each withheld paragraph, in 20 notes with the paragraph's number (Vietnam Hoa Hao 38 against 4;
  Bangladesh documentation 28/3; Syria religious minorities 20/4; Pakistan Ahmadis 19/6). The PDF therefore shows
  how many paragraphs were withheld and which numbers they had.
- Wording of the notice differs in 2 notes ("in this section" against "on this page": Kenya SOGIE, Georgia
  SOGIE).

### 9. Front and back matter

- **Version and date.** 140 notes show a version number in both; all 140 agree. 13 (bulletins and mission
  reports) have a reference and date instead, the same in both. 11 web versions have no version number at the top
  (it is still in the Clearance section at the end); 10 more give the version without the month. The "valid from"
  date agrees in all 157 notes where both give one, and the clearance version in all 153. Two notes contradict themselves in both
  versions alike (Nigeria separatists: 4.0 on the cover, 7.0 under Clearance; Ukraine security: 2.0 and 1.0).
- **"Section updated" lines.** 416 in the PDFs (136 notes), 389 on the web (126 notes). Same dates in the same
  order in 151 notes. Web has none of the PDF's in 10 (India religious minorities 11 lines; Bangladesh women 6; Iraq
  blood feuds 3; Brazil OCGs, Iran social media, Iraq religious minorities, Libya humanitarian, Sri Lanka minority
  religious groups, Sri Lanka SOGIE, Uganda SOGIE 1 each); China medical has 24 of 25. Different date: Georgia SOGIE
  and Iraq actors of protection.
- **"Changes from last version".** Syria criticism of the government differs (table below).
- **Cover, contents, page numbers.** PDF only: a cover, a contents list in all 164, 9,578 "Page x of y" footers,
  7,032 "Back to Contents" lines. The web body has none of these, except 6 stray "Back to Contents" lines in 5
  notes. A page reference can only be given from the PDF.
- **Contact details.** Same addresses; the web prints the email address where the PDF prints the team's name.
- The web page's "updated" date is the country page's, not the note's (all Iraq notes show 24 July 2026), so it
  cannot show which version was edited last.

### 10. Lists

- 11,167 list items in the web text (bibliographies excluded). 9,722 of the bulleted ones are bullets in the PDF;
  156 are plain paragraphs in the extraction and 54 web paragraphs are bullets in the PDF (both partly the
  extractor's misses: its bullet detection was not verified item by item).
- Nesting, where both are lists: same depth in 9,187 items, different in 535 (the extractor infers depth from
  indentation; not verified against the page).
- Nothing here changes what the text says.

### 11. Emphasis

The web versions carry almost no bold or italic (42 bold and 24 italic runs, in 6 notes). The PDFs' text has bold
in every note (the extractor marks 5,224 runs); what share is emphasis within a sentence, and what share
sub-headings and table headers, was not established.

## Extraction artefacts found (ours, to fix)

1,058 of the 3,785 differences were the extraction's, 6,885 words. None is a difference between the publications.
Items 1, 2(a), 4, 5 and 6 were tested again on the live `pdftext.py` as at 19:11 and are still present; item 7 is
fixed; the others were not re-tested.

1. **`figures_missing_from_web` reports pictures as missing that the web has.** It matches each block to the
   next web block with the same text; a repeated block (the redaction notice, "Back to Contents"-like boilerplate)
   makes it leap ahead, after which nothing matches and every later picture is declared missing. Reproduce: Syria
   security situation (July 2025) has 11 pictures in both versions, at the same places; the function returns 11
   missing, all placed after "Official – sensitive: Not for disclosure – End of section". Over all pairs it reports
   316 missing where the surplus is 220; 23 pairs are wrong, 14 of them with equal counts (Ukraine security 9,
   Ethiopia Tigrayans 8, Syria Kurds 7, Namibia women 6, Iran Kurds 6). The site would show these as left out by the
   web. Fix: align the two sequences (difflib, or anchors unique on both sides) instead of taking the next match.
2. **Footnote marks read as text: 240 places in 82 notes; 415 footnotes (88 notes) get no mark.** (a) Two marks together
   stay as raised text: Turkey Gülenist movement p. 14, "academia33,34." gives `academia<sup>33,34</sup>.`; also
   "70 71 72 73 74" (Iran security bulletin p. 10) and "fire178179180181" (Bangladesh religious minorities p. 44).
   (b) A mark at the end of a paragraph before a table or picture becomes a paragraph of its own ("268", Ghana
   internal relocation p. 66). (c) A mark on linked text stays glued to the word ("Medicines31", India medical).
3. **Tables with wrapped cells are scrambled: 412 places in 50 notes**, and 255 more places (29 notes) where words
   sit in the wrong place, nearly all in tables. Cell text is read line by line across the row, so a cell's words
   land in its neighbours (Vietnam opposition Annex A, pp. 44–51; Vietnam ethnic and religious groups §14; Vietnam
   Hoa Hao 9.3.10). 12 tables are not recognised and come out as paragraphs.
4. **Hollow "o" bullets set in Arial stay as a letter "o"**: 61 list items (Nigeria FGM 37, Vietnam mental
   healthcare 19, Albania mental healthcare 5; p. 25 there: "o Law and policy on mental health"). The rule accepts
   "o" only in Courier.
5. **Contents-list lines left in: 5 notes.** The last entries of a contents list on a page with fewer than three
   entries (DRC opposition: "Feedback to the Home Office ….. 32"), and the first line of an entry that wraps (Brazil
   actors of protection: "11.3 The Public Prosecutor’s Office (MP) and National Council of the Public").
6. **The version line loses the day**: "Version 7.0, 18 December 2025" on the cover becomes "Version 7.0, December
   2025" (Iran illegal exit; Syria religious minorities "11 February 2026").
7. Cover text under the title (a bulletin's "Reference / Summary", a mission's "Conducted between…"): 9 notes in
   the snapshot; fixed since.
8. **Chart lettering read as text** where a chart is not recognised: Libya security 9.4.23 p. 44 ("150 Fatalities
   100 50 0 2018 2019…"), India religious minorities 13.2.6 p. 52 ("2009 2010 … 2023").
9. **Arabic text is corrupted** by ligature expansion: "الاتصالات" comes out as "االتصاالت" (Iraq opposition in the
   Kurdistan region, p. 65).
10. Text lost, moved or mis-joined at page ends and in two-column lists: 125 places, 1,239 words, about 55 notes
    (a paragraph number dropped, Sri Lanka medical 6.8.1 p. 46; bibliography lines; the 20-item list in China
    Christians 12.1.3).
11. The check in `pdftext.check` counts `<li>` and headings, which the web's bibliography layout inflates; its
    five-word-phrase score cannot tell a real difference from an artefact, which is how 90.6% was read as a
    difference.

## Limits

- **Current editions only**, as stored on 2–3 October 2026. Earlier editions and the eleven PDF-only notes were not
  compared.
- **Text layer, not appearance.** The PDF side is the text a program can read. A word present in the picture of a
  page but absent from its text layer would be missed (none was noticed); pictures were counted, not compared, so a
  picture that differs between the two versions would not be found.
- **Which version is right** was not established, nor which the Home Office edits first. The web page's "updated"
  date does not help.
- **The extractor snapshot** is "pdftext-2" as at 18:38; later changes (cover text, JPEG figures) are not in the
  figures above and affect only the artefact counts.
- **Unresolved:** 25 differences (28 web words, 86 PDF words), mostly two-column numbered lists and Arabic text,
  could be placed in neither class.
- **Classification is mechanical.** The kinds (wording, picture-related, numbering…) come from rules, checked by
  reading several hundred cases but not all 2,671. A picture description the rules missed is counted as wording
  (this overstates wording, if anything). The table of significant differences was read and checked by hand; the
  other counts were not.
- **Short differences in repeated text.** A changed word whose neighbours also occur elsewhere in the PDF with the
  web's wording would be taken for an artefact. Paragraph numbers were given a stricter test for this reason.
- **Tables read as bags of words** (where the PDF's reading order differs) could hide a changed figure.
- **Not confirmed against the raw PDF one by one:** punctuation-only and paragraph-break differences; list nesting;
  heading levels; bold and italic; whether a web table with no PDF text counterpart corresponds to a particular
  picture.
- **Links** were compared as addresses; whether they work is a separate check (`./cpin links`).

## Since this review (3 October 2026, evening)

**The comparison is now a command.** `./cpin compare` (`src/cpin/webpdf.py`, method "webpdf-1") does what the
scripts behind this review did: the same diff, the same check of every difference against the PDF's raw text,
the same kinds. It keeps one record per note in `data/pdfs/compare/<pdf sha256>.json` (164 files, 1.3 MB) and
prints the standard table of `docs/methods/pdf-and-web.md`. Run on the audit's own inputs it gives the audit's
figures group for group, with one correction (below). Tests: `tests/test_webpdf.py`.

**One correction to the method.** The audit counted how often a passage occurs with a function that takes two
occurrences set end to end for one. A citation the web repeats in the footnote where the PDF gives a different
source was therefore sometimes classed as ours. Counted properly on the audit's own inputs, 155 more web-only
words are real, in 10 notes (Honduras gangs 36, India religious minorities 26, Bangladesh political situation 22,
