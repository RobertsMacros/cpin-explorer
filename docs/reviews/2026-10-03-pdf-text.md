# Text from PDFs: how well the extraction does (3 October 2026)

Some editions are published as a PDF only. The owner asked for them to be read "as you would any other", and
whether PDFs bring OCR problems. This records what was built and how it was measured.

## What is read, and how

`src/cpin/pdftext.py` (`./cpin pdftext`) takes the text from the PDF's own text layer and lays it out the way
GOV.UK lays out the web version of a note. Nothing is read by OCR and no language model is involved.

- **No OCR was needed.** All 175 PDFs held are made in Word and carry their text. A PDF with no text layer (a scan)
  is refused and stays a PDF with a link; none has been met.
- **Headings** come from the PDF's bookmarks (title, level, page). Five of the 164 PDFs checked have none (short
  bulletins): there, headings are found from type size, bold lines standing alone, and numbering. The same is done
  for pages before the first bookmark: older notes open with a Preface that has no bookmark.
- **Paragraphs** are lines closer together than a paragraph gap. A paragraph cut by a page end is rejoined when the
  line before was full: Word only breaks a line when the next word will not fit.
- **Footnotes** are the small type under the short rule at the foot of each page, matched to the raised numbers in
  the text and gathered at the end, as GOV.UK does.
- **Links** are the PDF's link rectangles matched to the characters inside them. **Lists** are lines that open with a
  bullet glyph. **Tables** come from PyMuPDF's table finder. **Figures** (pictures and drawn charts) are rendered as
  images (a photograph as a JPEG, anything else as a PNG). The cover's title and picture, the contents list, page
  numbers and "Back to Contents" are left out; what a cover says under its title (a bulletin's reference and summary,
  a mission's dates) is kept, as GOV.UK's web version keeps it.

The result is kept in `data/pdfs/text/` (committed, with its figures), separate from the stored bodies, and the
export adds it as an edition with `source: pdf`. The site shows such an edition as "From the PDF", links the PDF, and
says the layout is a reconstruction.

## The measurement

164 current reports have both a web version and a PDF. `./cpin pdftext --check` extracts each PDF and compares it
with GOV.UK's web version of the same edition (3.81 million words). Wording is compared as five-word phrases, with
tables and figures left out of that comparison (GOV.UK often gives a chart's numbers as a table the PDF does not have).

| Measure | First run | After the day's fixes (below) |
|---|---|---|
| Web version's wording found in the extraction | mean 99.1%, median 99.6% | mean 99.3%, median 99.6% |
| Reports at 99% or better / 95% or better / below 90% | 138 / 158 / 0 of 164 (worst 90.6%) | 145 / 161 / 0 (worst 93.3%) |
| Worst tenth of reports, from | 97.0% | 98.9% |
| Extraction's wording found in the web version | mean 99.2%, median 99.6% (worst 91.7%) | mean 99.4%, median 99.7% (worst 91.7%) |
| Footnotes: same count as the web version | 152 of 164 (within two in 160) | the same |
| Extractions that failed | 0 | 0 |

**This score tests the extractor. It is not a measure of how the web version and the PDF differ**, and was wrongly
read as one on the first run ("up to 10% different"). It cannot tell a real difference from a fault of ours. The
real differences are measured by `./cpin compare` (`docs/methods/pdf-and-web.md`,
`docs/reviews/2026-10-03-pdf-vs-web.md`): the middle note differs by five words.

The two versions are not identical to begin with (the Home Office edits one without the other, and the web versions
add tables for charts), so 100% is not the ceiling. Where the extraction falls short, the usual causes are:

- **Tables.** The lowest scores are healthcare notes dense with tables (Ghana 90.6%, Bangladesh 93.1%, China 94.0%).
  A table that runs over a page comes out as two tables; a table PyMuPDF does not recognise comes out as paragraphs.
- **Charts.** A drawn chart is kept as a picture, so its numbers are not text. GOV.UK's web version often adds a
  table of the same numbers, which the PDF does not contain.
- **Structure GOV.UK adds.** The web versions mark bibliography entries as lists under a heading for each source;
  the PDF has them as plain indented lines. The words are the same; the list and heading counts differ (which is why
  "same count" is low for headings and list items, and not a measure of lost text).

## Faults found the same day, and fixed

The owner questioned the 90.6%. Reading the worst pairs word by word against the PDFs' raw text showed the
shortfall was the extractor's, in these ways, each now fixed with a test:

- **An unbookmarked Preface was skipped** (600 to 830 words in about ten older notes: Ghana, Albania, Bangladesh and
  China medical notes, Russia, Nigeria, Jamaica, Ethiopia). Reading now starts straight after the cover's title.
- **A bulletin's cover text** ("Reference", "Summary") and a mission's ("Conducted between ...") were left out.
- **The contents list** left behind the first line of an entry that ran over two lines, and its last entries when
  they fell on a page of their own.
- **A table with shaded cells** was cut into a row for every line of text, rejected as a table, and then taken for
  a picture (three Vietnam notes). Such a table is now put back together: its rows and its columns.
- **Footnote marks set together** ("33,34", "70 71 72", "178179180") stayed as raised text.
- **The hollow bullet "o"** was recognised only in Courier; it is now taken in any typeface where a list item can
  begin (not where a wrapped line of Portuguese opens with the word "o").
- **The cover's date lost its day** ("18 December 2025" became "December 2025").
- **The cover's picture** (the department's logo) was taken as a figure in a PDF without bookmarks. A cover now
  gives text only.

Still open, listed in `docs/reviews/2026-10-03-pdf-vs-web.md`: a footnote mark at the end of a paragraph before a
table, or on linked text; tables with wrapped cells that are not shaded; chart lettering read as text where a chart
is not recognised; Arabic ligatures; text mis-joined at some page ends and in two-column lists.

## The editions this serves (on 3 October 2026)

Eleven reports' current editions, all with text layers: Albania (fact-finding mission, human trafficking), Brazil
(sexual orientation, gender identity and expression), France (safe third country), Gambia (sexual orientation and
gender identity), Ghana (sexual orientation, gender identity and expression), India (political parties and
affiliation), Palestine (humanitarian situation in Gaza), Sri Lanka and Vietnam (fact-finding missions), Zimbabwe
(opposition to the government; medical treatment and healthcare). Seven of these were not listed on the site before.

## Limits to keep in mind

- These eleven have no web version to check against. The figures above are for other reports' PDFs made the same
  way; they say how the extractor does in general, not that any one extraction is right.
- Figures have no description (GOV.UK's own images have none either).
- Older PDFs recovered from the Internet Archive may use earlier templates: look at a sample by eye before
  trusting them, and compare any that also have a web version.
- Change the extractor only with `./cpin pdftext --check` run before and after, and bump `EXTRACTOR` so stored
  extractions are redone.
